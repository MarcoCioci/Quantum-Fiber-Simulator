% APPENDIX A — Reduced Density Operators and Global-State Comparison

\chapter{Explicit Derivation of Reduced Density Operators and Comparison of Global States}
\label{app:reduced_density_operators}

This appendix provides an explicit derivation of the reduced density operators associated with the phase-evolved Bell state introduced in Chapter~3. It also illustrates an important conceptual point: identical local reduced states may correspond to physically different global states.

\medskip

The discussion clarifies why local measurements alone are insufficient to characterize bipartite quantum systems and motivates the introduction of global quantities such as purity, correlation functions, and entanglement measures.

%========================================================
% SECTION A.1 — Phase-Evolved Bell State
%========================================================

\section{Phase-Evolved Bell State}

We consider the two-qubit state

\begin{equation}
|\psi(\theta)\rangle
=
\frac{
|01\rangle
+
e^{i\theta}|10\rangle
}{
\sqrt{2}
},
\label{eq:appendix_phase_bell_state}
\end{equation}

written in the computational basis

\[
\{
|00\rangle,
|01\rangle,
|10\rangle,
|11\rangle
\}.
\]

Its vector representation is

\begin{equation}
|\psi(\theta)\rangle
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\begin{pmatrix}
0 \\
1 \\
e^{i\theta} \\
0
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:appendix_phase_bell_vector}
\end{equation}

The corresponding density operator is

\begin{equation}
\rho_{AB}(\theta)
=
|\psi(\theta)\rangle
\langle\psi(\theta)|.
\label{eq:appendix_phase_density_operator}
\end{equation}

Expanding explicitly gives

\begin{equation}
\rho_{AB}(\theta)
=
\frac{1}{2}
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & e^{-i\theta} & 0 \\
0 & e^{i\theta} & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:appendix_phase_density_matrix}
\end{equation}

% SECTION A.2 — Explicit Partial Trace
%========================================================

\section{Explicit Evaluation of the Partial Trace}

The reduced density operator of subsystem \( A \) is obtained through the partial trace over subsystem \( B \):

\begin{equation}
\rho_A(\theta)
=
\mathrm{Tr}_B
\left(
\rho_{AB}(\theta)
\right).
\label{eq:appendix_partial_trace_definition}
\end{equation}

Using the computational basis of subsystem \( B \),

\[
\{
|0\rangle,
|1\rangle
\},
\]

the partial trace can be written explicitly as

\begin{equation}
\rho_A(\theta)
=
\sum_{b=0}^{1}
\left(
I_A \otimes \langle b|
\right)
\rho_{AB}(\theta)
\left(
I_A \otimes |b\rangle
\right).
\label{eq:appendix_partial_trace_explicit}
\end{equation}

We now evaluate the individual contributions.

\medskip

The diagonal terms give

\begin{align}
\mathrm{Tr}_B
\left(
|01\rangle\langle 01|
\right)
&=
|0\rangle\langle 0|,
\nonumber
\\
\mathrm{Tr}_B
\left(
|10\rangle\langle 10|
\right)
&=
|1\rangle\langle 1|.
\label{eq:appendix_partial_trace_diagonal}
\end{align}

The off-diagonal terms vanish:

\begin{align}
\mathrm{Tr}_B
\left(
|01\rangle\langle 10|
\right)
&=
0,
\nonumber
\\
\mathrm{Tr}_B
\left(
|10\rangle\langle 01|
\right)
&=
0,
\label{eq:appendix_partial_trace_offdiagonal}
\end{align}

due to the orthogonality relation

\[
\langle 0|1\rangle = 0.
\]

Summing all contributions yields

\begin{equation}
\rho_A(\theta)
=
\frac{1}{2}
|0\rangle\langle 0|
+
\frac{1}{2}
|1\rangle\langle 1|
=
\frac{I}{2}.
\label{eq:appendix_reduced_density_A}
\end{equation}

By symmetry,

\begin{equation}
\rho_B(\theta)
=
\frac{I}{2}.
\label{eq:appendix_reduced_density_B}
\end{equation}

Thus, although the global state depends explicitly on the phase \( \theta \), the reduced states do not. The phase appears only in the off-diagonal coherence terms of the global density operator, which are eliminated by the partial trace.

\medskip

In particular, for \( \theta = 0 \),

\begin{equation}
|\Psi^+\rangle
=
\frac{
|01\rangle
+
|10\rangle
}{
\sqrt{2}
},
\label{eq:appendix_standard_bell_state}
\end{equation}

one obtains the same reduced states,

\begin{equation}
\rho_A
=
\rho_B
=
\frac{I}{2}.
\label{eq:appendix_standard_bell_reduced}
\end{equation}

% SECTION A.3 — Local Equivalence and Global Distinction
%========================================================

\section{Local Equivalence and Global Distinction}

The previous result illustrates an important conceptual property of bipartite quantum systems.

\medskip

Consider the classical mixture

\begin{equation}
\rho^{(\mathrm{class})}
=
\frac{1}{2}
|0\rangle\langle 0|
+
\frac{1}{2}
|1\rangle\langle 1|
=
\frac{I}{2}.
\label{eq:appendix_classical_mixture}
\end{equation}

At the local level,

\begin{equation}
\rho_A^{(\mathrm{ent})}
=
\rho^{(\mathrm{class})},
\label{eq:appendix_local_equivalence}
\end{equation}

and therefore, for any observable \( O_A \),

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Tr}
\left(
\rho^{(\mathrm{class})} O_A
\right)
=
\mathrm{Tr}
\left(
\rho_A^{(\mathrm{ent})} O_A
\right).
\label{eq:appendix_local_expectation_equivalence}
\end{equation}

Hence, local measurements cannot distinguish classical mixtures from reductions of entangled states.

\medskip

The distinction emerges only at the global level.

Consider the separable mixed state

\begin{equation}
\rho_{AB}^{(\mathrm{sep})}
=
\frac{1}{2}
|01\rangle\langle 01|
+
\frac{1}{2}
|10\rangle\langle 10|.
\label{eq:appendix_separable_state}
\end{equation}

Its reduced states are identical to those of the entangled Bell state:

\begin{align}
\mathrm{Tr}_B
\left(
\rho_{AB}^{(\mathrm{sep})}
\right)
&=
\frac{I}{2},
\nonumber
\\
\mathrm{Tr}_A
\left(
\rho_{AB}^{(\mathrm{sep})}
\right)
&=
\frac{I}{2}.
\label{eq:appendix_separable_reductions}
\end{align}

However, the separable state lacks the coherence terms present in the entangled density operator.

\medskip

This difference becomes visible through global quantities.

For the entangled state,

\begin{equation}
\gamma
\left(
\rho_{AB}(\theta)
\right)
=
1,
\label{eq:appendix_entangled_purity}
\end{equation}

whereas for the separable mixture,

\begin{equation}
\gamma
\left(
\rho_{AB}^{(\mathrm{sep})}
\right)
=
\frac{1}{2}.
\label{eq:appendix_separable_purity}
\end{equation}

A second distinction appears in bipartite correlations.

For the entangled phase-evolved state,

\begin{align}
\langle
\sigma_x \otimes \sigma_x
\rangle
&=
\cos\theta,
\nonumber
\\
\langle
\sigma_y \otimes \sigma_y
\rangle
&=
\cos\theta,
\label{eq:appendix_entangled_correlations}
\end{align}

whereas for the separable mixture,

\begin{align}
\langle
\sigma_x \otimes \sigma_x
\rangle
&=
0,
\nonumber
\\
\langle
\sigma_y \otimes \sigma_y
\rangle
&=
0.
\label{eq:appendix_separable_correlations}
\end{align}

\medskip

Thus, states with identical reduced density operators may differ fundamentally at the global level.

Reduced states encode all locally accessible information, but do not capture global coherence or bipartite entanglement.
